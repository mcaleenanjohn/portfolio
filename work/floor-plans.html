Case study 01  ·  HoneyBook

Floor plans

HoneyBook's first native floor planning tool, built for its highest-value vertical.

RoleEnd-to-end product design
TimelineApr – Jun 2026
TeamProduct, Engineering, Venue support
CompanyHoneyBook

Overview

Venues are one of HoneyBook's highest-revenue verticals — and the ones most likely to need a floor plan for every event they book.

Without a native tool, they were cobbling together workflows across Canva, Prismm, and AllSeated. HoneyBook had the booking data, the client portal, and the relationship. It was missing the tool.

Venues book more events per year than almost any other member type, and were also the segment most at risk — Prismm and Social Tables were actively targeting them, and floor planning was HoneyBook's biggest gap.

The problem

01

Every plan was built from scratch, exported, emailed, revised, and sent again. Hours of work per booking.

02

A separate platform subscription just to fill a gap HoneyBook should have owned.

03

No shared source of truth — clients, coordinators, and staff often worked from different versions of the same plan.

04

Disconnected from the contract, guest count, and client portal — context HoneyBook already had.

My role

End-to-end product design: competitive research, user journey mapping, UX/UI, design QA, and V2 scoping.

Objective

Build a native floor planning tool inside the HoneyBook project workflow, connected to CRM data competitors don't have.

Research

A competitive analysis across 13 tools, a full mapping of the venue workflow, and interviews with early users.

Workspace UX

Floor plans live inside the project, not off in another tool.

Before designing the builder itself, I mapped out how floor plans would live inside a HoneyBook project: a three-status model that separates prepping from sharing, two distinct experiences for venues and clients, and a create flow that surfaces templates from the start.

The builder — V1

Scoped as an MVP on purpose.

We knew V1 wasn't the full picture, but we wanted it in front of real users fast — so we could learn from how they actually built plans and refine from there. It launched to 30% of venue members: an element panel organized by category, a status bar, an infinite canvas with a scale indicator, and a way to calibrate the grid to real-world dimensions.

Member feedback

11 members responded. The signal was clear.

We launched to 30% of venue members and reached out for feedback right away. Real dimensions and scale were the single biggest reason people weren't switching from their current tools.

63%
No real room dimensions or scale — the biggest blocker to switching.
54%
Wanted to start from a blueprint upload, not draw walls from scratch.
45%
Missing object types — dance floors, bars, podiums, screens.
36%
Wanted templates: save a layout once, reuse it per booking.
27%
The wall tool's interaction model didn't match expectations.
18%
Items could accidentally move, with no way to lock them in place.

V2 improvements

Turning feedback into product direction.

I helped align Product, Engineering, and the venue team around what to ship first: templates so venues could reuse a layout across bookings, real-world scale so a table is actually 60 inches, and a rebuilt wall tool with a clearer interaction model. Through it all I partnered with engineering to keep the experience connected to HoneyBook's project workflow — not a bolted-on standalone tool.
