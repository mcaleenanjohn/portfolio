Overview

Venues are one of HoneyBook's highest-revenue verticals—and the customers most likely to need a floor plan for every event.

Without a native tool, venue teams were piecing the workflow together across Canva, Prismm, and AllSeated. HoneyBook already had the booking data, client portal, and relationship. It was missing the tool.

Floor planning was a completely separate job.

Build a plan in another tool, export it, email it, get feedback, update it, and send it again. Different people ended up working from different versions—with no connection to the contract, guest count, or client portal.

The opportunity: bring floor planning into the project itself, creating one shared source of truth.

Before designing the builder, I designed where it would live.

Floor Plans became a dedicated tab inside every HoneyBook project.

I defined three statuses to separate preparation from sharing, distinct experiences for venues and clients, and a creation flow that surfaced templates from the start.

Version control was the real problem—not drawing.

We studied 13 tools, mapped the venue workflow end-to-end, and spoke with early users.

The biggest pain wasn't creating a floor plan. It was knowing which version was current.

We also found that venues work in two modes: set up a space once, then adapt it for each booking. Most existing tools didn't account for that.

And HoneyBook had something competitors didn't: the context around the plan—guest counts, contracts, clients, and relationships—already lived in the product.

We deliberately started small.

The first version wasn't meant to solve everything.

We wanted to get a working product in front of real venues, learn from how they actually used it, and use that feedback to shape what came next.

V1 focused on the core builder: a categorized element library, infinite canvas, ruler, and image-scale calibration. It launched to 30% of venue members.

I would LOVE to see a floor plan feature that could be used on the platform successfully.

The feedback gave us a clear path forward.

I helped align Product, Engineering, and the venue team around what to ship first—and what would make the product genuinely competitive.

Align the MVP
Focus V1 on the core builder so we could launch quickly and learn.

Set the V2 priorities
Turn member feedback into a roadmap centered on scale, blueprint uploads, templates, and precision.

Protect the product vision
Partner through build and QA to keep Floor Plans native to the HoneyBook project—not a standalone tool.
