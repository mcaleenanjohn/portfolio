Case study 01

Floor plans

HoneyBook's first native floor planning tool, built for its highest-value vertical.

Role
End-to-end product design
Timeline
Apr – Jun 2026
Team
Product, Engineering, Venue support
Company
HoneyBook

Overview

Venues are one of HoneyBook's highest-revenue verticals — and the one most likely to need a floor plan for every event they book.

Without a native tool, they were cobbling together workflows across Canva, Prismm, and AllSeated. HoneyBook had the booking data, the client portal, and the relationship. It was missing the tool.

Venues book more events per year than almost any other member type — more projects, more clients, more revenue per account. They were also the segment most at risk: Prismm and Social Tables were actively targeting them, and floor planning was HoneyBook's biggest gap. The company responded by assigning a dedicated support team to the vertical and building the tool they needed most.

My role

End-to-end product design: competitive research, user journey mapping, UX/UI, design QA, and V2 scoping.

Objective

Build a native floor planning tool inside the HoneyBook project workflow, connected to CRM data competitors don't have — and give venues a reason to stop looking elsewhere.

Research & findings

A competitive analysis across 13 tools, the full venue workflow mapped end to end, and interviews with early users to understand where things were breaking down.

  • Version confusion was the biggest pain.Nobody knew which PDF was current. Clients had one version, coordinators had another, staff had a printed copy from two weeks ago.
  • Venues think in two modes.Set a space up once, then adjust it per booking. Most tools didn't account for that distinction.
  • HoneyBook had an edge nobody else had.Guest counts, contracts, client relationships — all the context a floor plan needs, already in the system.

Workspace UX

Before designing the builder, I mapped how floor plans would live inside a HoneyBook project. The key decisions: a three-status model that separates prepping from sharing, two distinct experiences for venues and clients, and a create flow that surfaces templates from the start — so venues aren't rebuilding the same room every booking.

The builder — V1

V1 was intentionally scoped as an MVP. We knew it wasn't the full picture, but we wanted it in front of real users fast — to learn from how they actually built plans and refine from there. It launched to 30% of venue members.

  1. Element panel. Furniture, shapes, rooms, and text, organized by category. Drag or click to place.
  2. Status bar. Floor plan name, project, and current publish status at a glance.
  3. Canvas. Infinite grid with a 1'×1' scale indicator. Build freely, set scale once.
  4. Set scale. Calibrate the grid to match real-world room dimensions.

Member feedback

We launched to 30% of venue members and reached out right away. 11 responded. The signal was clear.

  • No real room dimensions or scale 63%The single biggest reason people weren't switching from their current tools.
  • No blueprint / template upload 54%Members wanted to start from a fixed layout, not draw walls from scratch every time.
  • Missing object types 45%Dance floors, bars, podiums, screens — the library was too sparse for real event use.
  • Templates & reusability 36%Save a layout once, reuse it per booking.
  • Wall tool is confusing 27%The interaction model didn't match expectations.
  • Items can move accidentally 18%No way to lock elements in place.

Turning feedback into direction

I helped align Product, Engineering, and the venue team around what to ship first — and what would make the product genuinely competitive.

  • Aligned the MVP.Focused V1 on the core builder so we could launch quickly and learn from real venue workflows.
  • Set the V2 priorities.Turned member feedback into a clear roadmap centered on scale, blueprint uploads, templates, and precision.
  • Protected the product vision.Partnered with engineering through build and QA to keep the experience connected to HoneyBook's project workflow — not a standalone tool.
V2 improvements

What shipped next

Templates

One of the most requested features. Venues wanted to build a room once and reuse it across every booking, without starting from scratch.

Scale & dimensions

7 of 11 members said scale was the single biggest reason they couldn't switch. We fixed it.

  • Upload a blueprint, then set scale — calibrate the canvas to the venue's real floor plan.
  • Rulers on canvas that update as you build, always showing real measurements.
  • Live dimensions on any selected element — no more guessing whether a table is actually 60 inches.

Control & precision

The rest of the work made the builder feel more precise and polished.

  • Rebuilt wall tool — click, drag, click, with an auto-ruler showing each segment's length as you draw.
  • Measure tool — drag to check any distance on the canvas in real-world units.
  • Scale legend fixed to the canvas, always visible and in sync with the rulers.

Final design
