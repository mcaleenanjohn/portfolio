Overview

Venues are one of HoneyBook's highest-revenue verticals, and the one most likely to need a floor plan for every event — but there was no native tool. Venue teams pieced it together across Canva, Prismm, and AllSeated. HoneyBook had the booking data, the client portal, and the relationship. It was missing the tool.

The problem

Venues were also the segment most at risk — Prismm and Social Tables were courting them directly. The objective: a native floor-planning tool inside the project workflow, wired to the CRM data competitors don't have.

Version control, not drawing, was the real problem.

Clients, coordinators, and venue staff each worked from a different PDF, exported from a different tool — and nobody was ever sure which one was current. Set-up also happens in two modes: build a space once, then adjust it per booking. And most of the context a plan needs — guest counts, contracts, client history — already lived in HoneyBook.

V1 was intentionally scoped as an MVP.

We wanted it in front of real users fast — to learn how they actually build.

Floor plans had to live inside the project, not another tab. I mapped the workspace first: a three-status model, distinct views for venues and clients, and a create flow that leads with templates. V1 itself focused on the canvas — a categorized element panel, an infinite canvas with a ruler, and image-scale calibration.

I would LOVE to see a floor plan feature that could be used on the platform successfully.
— Venue member

We shipped to 30%, then asked what was missing.

Eleven venue members responded within the week. Three needs dominated.

63%
named real dimensions and scale as the reason they hadn't switched from their current tool
54%
wanted to start from a blueprint upload, not draw every wall from scratch
45%
hit missing object types — dance floors, bars, podiums, screens

Templates and reuse, a clearer wall tool, and a way to lock elements in place rounded out the list.

Turning feedback into direction.

I helped align Product, Engineering, and the venue team around what to ship first — and what would make the product genuinely competitive.

We aligned on the MVP so we could launch and learn instead of guessing, and set the V2 priorities — scale, blueprint uploads, templates, precision — straight from the feedback. And we kept it native: I partnered through build and QA so Floor Plans stayed part of the project workflow, not a bolt-on.

Scale & dimensions

The number-one blocker. Upload the venue's real blueprint, set the scale, and canvas rulers track real measurements as you build; live dimensions appear on any selected element.

Precision

A rebuilt wall tool with a segment ruler, a measure tool, and a scale legend locked to the canvas.

Templates

Build a room once, reuse it across every booking — the most-requested feature from early users.

One place for every plan, inside the project.

No exports, no second subscription. One source of truth.

Launched with no marketing. Members found it themselves.
